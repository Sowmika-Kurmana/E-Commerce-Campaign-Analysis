SECTION 5

Statistical Hypothesis Testing

Two-Proportion Z-Test evaluating whether the observed difference is statistically distinguishable from zero or attributable to chance.

PARAMETRIC HYPOTHESIS TEST

Two-Proportion Z-Test

NOT STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT FAIL TO REJECT H₀
Null Hypothesis (H₀)

"There is no significant difference in conversion rates between Control and Treatment."

H₀: p_treatment - p_control = 0
Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

"There is a significant difference in conversion rates between Control and Treatment."

H₁: p_treatment - p_control ≠ 0
Significance Level (α) 0.05 95% Confidence (z_crit = ±1.96)
z-statistic -1.312 Inside fail-to-reject zone
p-value 0.18965 p-value (0.18965) > α (0.05)
Pooled Proportion (p_pool) 11.96% SE ≈ 0.001204

Standard Normal Distribution ($Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$) & Decision Regions

Visualizing the critical rejection thresholds (±1.96) versus the observed test statistic (z = -1.312).

Rejection Tails (α/2 = 0.025) p-value Area (0.18965)
Observed z = -1.312 lies between -1.96 and +1.96 STATUS: NOT STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT

Statistical Interpretation

"The observed difference in conversion rates is not statistically significant at the 5% significance level."

Formal Scientific Conclusion:

"The experiment does not provide sufficient statistical evidence that the Treatment landing page changed conversion performance."

Note: We do NOT claim that the Treatment landing page is definitively worse or that the redesign failed. We simply fail to reject the null hypothesis due to lack of evidence beyond natural variance.

SECTION 7

End-to-End Experimentation Lifecycle

Chronological stages executed throughout the IBM Q2D A/B testing analytics workflow.

1

1. Business Problem Definition

Identify business goals: Determine if the new landing page (new_page) drives higher purchasing conversions than the baseline (old_page).

2

2. Data Collection & Ingestion

Collect raw user interaction logs spanning user_id, timestamp, experiment group, landing page, and binary conversion status.

3

3. Data Cleaning & Integrity Auditing

Audit duplicate IDs, remove invalid group/page cross-assignments, and eliminate residual duplicate users to validate the dataset.

4

4. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Analyze user distributions across groups (Control 145,274 vs Treatment 145,311), compute conversion rates (12.04% vs 11.88%) and observed lift (-1.31%).

5

5. A/B Testing & Statistical Modeling

Formulate null and alternative hypotheses, establish significance threshold α = 0.05, compute pooled proportion (11.96%) and two-proportion z-statistic (z = -1.312).

6

6. Statistical Decision

Compare two-tailed p-value (0.18965) against α = 0.05 → FAIL TO REJECT H₀ (Not Statistically Significant).

7

7. Business Recommendation & Action

Recommend retaining existing landing page, preventing unnecessary engineering costs, and formulating new multivariate experiment hypotheses.

SECTION 8

Future Experimentation Opportunities

Structured roadmap for iterating upon the e-commerce campaign funnel to discover positive conversion lift.

Test New Landing-Page Designs

Iterate on hero layouts, product showcase hierarchy, and visual proof elements rather than single page swaps.

Test Headlines & CTAs

Conduct targeted A/B tests on value proposition copy, primary button colors, microcopy, and urgency cues.

Analyze Customer Segments

Disaggregate results by device type (mobile vs desktop), traffic source, geographic region, and returning vs new users.

Evaluate Revenue Per User

Expand beyond binary conversion rate to analyze Average Order Value (AOV) and Total Revenue per Visitor (RPV).

Calculate Confidence Intervals

Construct 95% Confidence Intervals for the true conversion rate difference [-0.0039, +0.0008] to quantify bounds.

Perform Statistical Power Analysis

Pre-calculate Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) and sample sizes required to achieve 80% or 90% statistical power.

Build Automated Experiment Monitoring

Deploy continuous real-time guardrail metrics, sample ratio mismatch (SRM) monitors, and sequential testing early stopping boundaries to protect business revenue during live tests.
